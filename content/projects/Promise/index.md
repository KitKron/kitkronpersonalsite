<title>A Broken Promise</title>

The tabletop radio sat in front of me, static erupting from its small speaker. It was old and 
overused. The yellowed numbers on the frequency dial were almost gone, save for a few chips of paint, 
and the metal accents on its sides were rusted red. I had been tuning it for quite some time now. I was 
trying to locate a signal. Where it was and what it sounded like I didn’t know, but I would find it 
eventually. Sometimes I could hear voices in the static. It was always hard to understand them. It didn’t 
seem like they were talking to me directly, more so like they were talking to someone else in the 
distance. Someone who wasn’t there anymore. I could never listen to them for long, the talking would 
only last a moment before sinking back in to the radio static. The signals were transitory, fading in and 
out in different places at different times. All I could do was turn the dial back and forth. The radio 
allowed me that power, and nothing more.
I searched between the frequencies. Muffled voices forced their way through the hissing from 
time-to-time. One emerged from the static, but something felt different about it. It was still 
unintelligible, but more constant. I could gather glimpses of words. “Happy… long… lived… room… 
believe…” A woman sat across from me. Her appearance was erratic, her face and torso warped and 
opaque. A creature of fog. The room was similar. Colors danced around me as the wallpaper changed 
from columns of switchgrass to bushes of seaside roses to fields of mangroves. It was never right, yet it 
was always familiar. “I’m so happy we’re here together,” the woman said. Her voice was a symphony 
of others that spoke her words. Her lips were distinct, but the color of her lipstick shifted rapidly, first 
red then purple then brown. “The colors match really well.” She hadn’t said that now. That was from 
before. None of it was right. I covered my eyes. My head felt heavy. I just wanted to see her face.
I felt a hand on my arm. “We’ll figure it out.” Though she didn’t talk with her own voice, the 
words still comforted me. There were boxes around us, labeled and covered in shipping tape, some 
opened. Glasses of wine, or maybe beer, sat in front of both of us. She grabbed hers and held it up to 
me, then brought it to her mouth. Her lips were so clear. Her lipstick was colored a deep purple. It left a 
print on the glass. She always wore lipstick at odd times. She looked so pretty with it on.
“I’m so happy we’re here together. God knows it took us long enough.” I could hear her within 
the choir, but her voice overpowered by the others.
“I’m surprised that we could even get here. It never felt like it was in the cards but we did it!” I 
responded instinctively. The words I spoke were fuzzy, like someone had picked all the substance away 
from them, leaving only the bones.
“We really did. What should we do with all this space? This place is so much bigger than any of 
the others!” Her sentences were malformed. Something had been ripped away from them. My head 
throbbed in rhythm with her voice. The signal was weak. This was all it could provide.
“Maybe that room can be for you! Although I’m not really sure if you’d use it that much.” Tears 
started to form in my eyes.
“I’m honestly not sure either. Maybe it could be for you?” My body felt stiff. This wasn’t the 
signal I was looking for. It was too muffled.
“I don’t want to have to buy the equipment. Plus the membership—” That wasn’t what I said. 
Those words weren’t hers and these weren’t mine. Tears slid down my face. I couldn’t look at her 
anymore. She wasn’t there. The only thing that I could make out was her lips and that wasn’t enough. I 
needed all of her, but the radio wouldn’t allow it; it was too broken. The crackling static crashed in like 
tall waves. It got louder and louder.. The walls became fuzzy and speckled. The boxes were washed 
away with each thundering wave. I looked towards her but all I could see was static. It had devoured 
her. It filled my ears and my eyes and my head as it consumed me. This was the closest I had come to 
her. Maybe next time the signal would be right. Maybe I could see her again.
I took a moment to hold my head until my mind reformed itself. The static left in a tide until it 
no longer occupied the crevasses of my skull. I noticed a small crack in the radio’s side. I ran my hand 
over the split in the metal, flakes of rust covering the pads of my fingers. Each broken signal left its 
mark on the radio. Eventually there would be nothing left. I kept turning the dial; the signal was close 
now. The voices between the frequencies felt clearer than before, like they had finally acknowledged 
me. They were trying to reach out. A chorus of voices surfaced from the hissing yet again, their words 
rippling through the static. “Okay… called… exactly… safe… home…” She was crouching in front of 
me while I sat on a chair, or maybe a stool. She was more tangible now, her silhouette encased in an 
angelic shimmer. A creature of light. We were in an office of some kind. The walls shifted from plaster 
to windows to brick. She held my hands in hers, her face turned upwards at me. “I’m so sorry.” Her 
voice was no longer just a member of the choir, she now lead it. “Here, hold out your hand really 
quick.” She kept saying things from before. This still wasn’t right, but she felt close. She wanted to 
connect, but the signal was weak. I gazed into her eyes, black, then brown, then green. They were 
shallow and unfamiliar, but I could still see the ocean within them.
“I’m so glad you’re safe and okay.” There were shrouded figures all around us, concerned and 
scared. Creatures of dark. They whispered among themselves, their words indecipherable, their anxiety 
infectious. My heart pounded. I looked back to her, hoping to find comfort in her light. Her eyes were 
so clear, the irises navy blue. They glistening like sunlight through water.
“Are you okay? They called me as soon as they could.” The other voices started to melt into 
hers. She was so clear, but the connection was too poor; there were parts of her that had been lost.
“Yeah I’m alright. I don’t think it was a big deal but I guess they’re just being cautious.” Bits of 
my response had been omitted. My words were no longer fuzzy, but incomplete.
“Better safe than sorry, right? I’m just glad you’re okay.” She looked at me with a mixture of 
concern and relief. She clasped my hands tightly. “Do you know what happened?”
“Honestly I’m not totally sure. I was just trying to get some work done and then my brain just 
stopped working. I got confused and then just… scared. At least that’s what people told me. I don’t 
really remember.” My head was filled with holes. This was the best I could do.
“That’s…” Her eyes glanced down at the floor. For a brief moment, they were oceans. My heart 
began to beat heavily again. The whispers among the figures grew louder. She raised her eyes back up 
to mine. “Do you want to head home now? Your boss gave me the okay for you to leave early.” Even 
with the holes in her words, she comforted me.
“Yes please. I don’t feel super great.” I looked back at the figures as they conversed with each 
other. My head felt heavy.
“It’s gonna be okay. I’ll always be here.” Light refracted off her eyes.
“Thank you. I’ll always be here too.” The static flowed in again, but this time, it was a soft 
stream. It filled up the room until everything was washed away. I let it soak into me. I was close now. I 
would find the signal soon. I would be able to see her again.
Tide pools in my head slowly drained of the static. The radio’s dial had cracked and split into 
pieces, leaving only the metal knob underneath. I pinched it between my forefinger and thumb and kept 
searching. The connection was the clearest it had been. Voices flew by, screaming at me, begging for 
attention. I stopped. It was her voice. No longer was it a conglomeration, but a singular, melodic tone. 
She was talking directly to me. I responded, unhindered.
We sat on an old, rusted bench that overlooked a secluded beach. It was overcast and fog 
obscured anything distant. A cold wind knocked against us, forcing us to huddle together while we 
watched the ocean. I rested my head on her shoulder as she leaned into me. We listened to the sound of 
waves crashing upon hidden rocks beyond the fog. I broke the silence.
“I’ve always loved when its foggy. Makes it feel like you’re in a dream. I hope it feels like this 
all the time.” She turned her head to me as I lifted mine from her shoulder. She was wearing coral pink 
lipstick, even though it was just the two of us. Her navy blue eyes looked into mine for a moment, then 
out towards the ocean.
“I think I like it more when its sunny. It makes the entire beach seem alive. Plus the water looks 
pretty when the sun hits it. I like all the colors.”
“Okay, but what about when its all rainy and gloomy like this, you could see a rainbow! Those 
have all the colors you need!” She let out a small laugh.
“Okay true… but you do need sunlight for a rainbow.”
“Oh, yeah. I kinda forgot about that. Well regardless, it’s always peaceful, just in different 
ways.” She smiled at me for a moment, then looked back to the ocean.
“Why aren’t you scared?” Her tone was stern. I looked at the ocean too.
“I am scared!” I said slightly forcefully. “I guess I just got it into my head somehow that it’ll be 
like a living dream. And sure, some times you’ll have a nightmare, but all the other times you’ll be in 
places like this!” I gestured with my head towards the sea, then looked back at her. She was deep in 
thought. 
“I hope it’s like that. I’m just worried that I’m going to lose you to those dreams. I’ve been 
worried about it since that day I picked you up from work.” She wasn’t sad, not visibly. Her eyes 
glanced downwards for a moment.
“Hey! I made a promise to you that I’d always be here for you. I’m not about to break it.” My 
tone was lighthearted, but I meant it.
“Yeah, I know. I made the same promise to you though, so I’m not about to break it either.”
“Guess it’s solved then! Come on lets go see how cold the water is.” I stood up and looked for a 
good way to get down to the beach. I wanted her to stop thinking about it.
“Hey, lets make another promise together.” She clasped my hand as I started to walk away. I 
turned back to her and met her eyes with mine. Light refracted off them in a way that made the moment 
feel unreal. “Lets promise each other that, even when we’re dreaming, we’ll always be there for each 
other. Okay?” Her voice shook a little. She was scared. I was too.
“Okay. I promise.”
“I love you.”
“I love you too. Always.” I took her hand and tugged her down a trail that was concealed by tall 
stalks of grass. “I’m totally pushing you in.”
“Like you could! I’m like ten times stronger than you.”
“We’ll see about that.”
